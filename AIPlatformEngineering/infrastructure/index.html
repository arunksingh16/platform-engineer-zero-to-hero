10 lessons / Design, size, operate

AI infrastructure at scale

You know Kubernetes. Now learn why a running GPU Pod is only the beginning: a useful AI platform must fit the model, move data fast enough, meet deadlines and survive failures.

Your outcome

Defend an accelerator and cluster design, calculate explicit resource budgets, validate capacity with measurements, and explain your operating plan in a design review or interview.

Two systems, one engineering discipline

Training team

Train a dense language model on a fixed token budget before a deadline. Follow distributed execution, checkpoint recovery, scheduling and cost per completed run.

Inference team

Operate a coding assistant with variable demand and long prompts. Follow memory, serving replicas, latency, warm capacity and cost per successful request.

Both are fictional teaching cases. Calculator defaults and performance figures are invented, not hardware benchmarks. LLMs provide the worked examples; data delivery, topology, scheduling and failure planning also apply to other AI workloads. Image and multimodal workloads need their own memory and throughput measurements.

Before you begin

Know Pods, nodes, resource requests, Services and basic networking. No GPU or machine-learning background is required. Read explanations, work examples by hand, then change one variable in each explorer. Allow roughly 25–40 minutes per lesson, and longer for the capstones.

The SGLang versus vLLM lesson explains engine selection. Here we design the infrastructure around either engine. Existing engine learning paths provide implementation depth.

Learning path

    How to use this in an interview

    State the workload and targets first. Draw boundaries and data paths. Show your assumptions and arithmetic. Explain what you would benchmark, what fails, and what evidence would change your decision. A lab or this course supports your reasoning; describe actual production experience separately and honestly.
