Lesson 10

Capacity and failure design

Do the capacity calculation after removing a failure domain. "We have three zones" says nothing about whether the survivors can carry traffic.

By the end you can...

Budget surviving capacity, in-flight work, retries and network cost.

Start with measured safe capacity

Measure per-instance throughput at the latency/error SLO using a representative request mix. Peak benchmark throughput is not safe operating capacity. Six VMs across three zones, each safe at 500 requests/s, provide 3,000 requests/s normally and 2,000 after losing a two-VM zone. A 2,400 requests/s peak does not fit after that loss.

Remove one zone

Three zones, two VMs each, 500 requests/s safe capacity per VM. No spare startup capacity is assumed.

Requests are not the only budget

Little's Law gives average in-flight work L = arrival rate x average time in system in a stable system. At 2,000 requests/s and 0.2 s average time, that is 400 requests in flight. If latency rises to 2 s at the same admitted rate, it becomes 4,000. These are averages, not p99 limits or a prediction of an unstable queue.

Budget sockets, file descriptors, conntrack entries, SNAT ports, TLS handshakes, packets/s and bandwidth separately. Autoscaling cannot instantly replace lost capacity: detection, VM startup, registration and warm-up take time. Cloud LB scaling and quotas also need review for sudden events.

Retries amplify incidents

If each of three nested call layers makes up to three attempts, one original operation can produce up to 3 x 3 x 3 = 27 calls to the deepest service. Retry only suitable failures within an end-to-end deadline, use exponential backoff and jitter, and enforce a retry budget. Writes may need idempotency keys; a timeout does not prove the write never happened.

Use admission control, bounded queues, circuit breaking and deliberate load shedding. A degraded catalog response can be better than exhausting every thread. For persistent clients, stagger reconnects and test a proxy/zone loss with realistic connection counts.

The network bill follows the path

Compare LB processing/capacity charges, NAT data processing, inter-zone/inter-region transfer, internet egress, CDN delivery and logs. Cross-zone balancing may help availability while changing traffic paths and cost; provider and service charging rules differ. Trace bytes across each hop rather than assuming a free private path.

Do it by hand

How many 500 requests/s VMs per zone, evenly spread across three zones, are needed to sustain 2,400 requests/s after any one zone fails?

Show answer

Two surviving zones x n VMs x 500 must be at least 2,400. n must be at least 2.4, so use at least 3 VMs per zone, 9 total, before additional safety margin. At 2,400 requests/s, surviving utilization is 80% of the stated safe capacity.
