Storage and tested recovery

A Pod can be rescheduled. Its data may be tied to a zone, an attachment, a key or a consistency point. Treat recovery as a data workflow, not a YAML replay.

By the end you can…

Choose storage from workload semantics, diagnose a topology-bound claim and design a restore test with measurable RPO and RTO.

“ReadWriteOnce” means read-write attachment by a single node, not necessarily a single Pod. Several Pods on that node can still access such a volume. Where supported and required, ReadWriteOncePod provides single-Pod semantics. Neither mode makes the database application safe for concurrent writers. Shared filesystem support is not permission to run an unmodified single-writer database on multiple replicas.

Binding is also scheduling

Inspect volumeBindingMode. With WaitForFirstConsumer, provisioning can account for the selected Pod's topology instead of binding a volume before placement constraints are known. A zonal disk still constrains future placement. Check the disk SKU and region's redundancy capabilities rather than claiming every Azure disk is either always zonal or automatically cross-zone.

A Pending PVC under delayed binding is not automatically a storage outage. Read the scheduling events. A mount failure may instead be a disk attachment limit, stale attachment, network path for Files or permissions. Do not force-detach a live writer without the storage runbook: two hosts believing they own the same data is worse than a temporarily Pending Pod.

Snapshots are a component, not a recovery strategy

A storage snapshot may be crash-consistent, not application-consistent. Databases can require their own backup and log replay, quiescing or coordination across volumes. Kubernetes object backups must include the objects needed to reconstruct the workload, while data backups must cover the persistent bytes. Secrets, external identity grants, private DNS and encryption keys also affect recoverability.

AKS Backup support varies by volume type, region and feature configuration. Check the current support matrix, retention, vault/tier behavior and cross-region capabilities before promising disaster recovery. CSI snapshots do not automatically mean every volume is included. Keep backups protected from the same credentials and failure domain that can destroy production.

A restore drill with an actual pass condition

  1. Write the RPO: maximum tolerated data loss, measured by recoverable transactions or time.
  2. Write the RTO: maximum time from disruption to verified usable service, including detection, provisioning and DNS.
  3. Restore into an isolated approved environment with required network, identity and keys, without letting restored jobs charge customers or send real notifications.
  4. Validate schema, integrity, transaction checkpoints and a representative application read/write flow.
  5. Measure elapsed recovery and data loss. Document missing dependencies and repeat until the objectives are met.

Retention is a separate safety boundary

Inspect a StorageClass/PV's reclaim policy before deleting a PVC. A Delete policy can remove the backing storage; Retain preserves it for manual reclamation but does not create a backup. Expansion also depends on class/driver support and filesystem behavior. Do not promise shrinking a persistent disk as an easy rollback.

Do it by hand: a zone outage

Application replicas can run in zones 1 and 2, but the database's only persistent disk is zonal in zone 1. Zone 1 is unavailable. Is a healthy zone-2 pool enough?

Follow the data

No. Compute in zone 2 cannot make an unavailable zone-1 disk attach there. Recovery needs an application replication/failover design or a supported data restore into eligible storage, plus tested consistency and network/identity dependencies. Three stateless replicas do not make the entire service zone-resilient.
