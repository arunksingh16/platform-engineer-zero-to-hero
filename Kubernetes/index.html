Kubernetes from zero

Kubernetes looks like a hundred unrelated features. It is closer to one idea applied a hundred times. Learn the control loop properly in lesson 1 and the autoscalers, the scheduler and operators all turn out to be the same thing pointed at different objects.

How each lesson works

  1. Idea in plain words with a tiny example.
  2. Play with an animation or interactive widget.
  3. Do it by hand — a short exercise with a hidden answer.
  4. Quiz — pass it to complete the lesson.

Lessons

    This course is deliberately vendor-neutral

    Everything here works the same on any conformant cluster — your laptop, a managed service, or bare metal. Where something is provider-specific it is named as such and left for the platform courses rather than quietly assuming AWS.

    Lesson 7 is the clearest case. Karpenter's NodePool and NodeClaim are vendor-neutral, but the NodeClass that actually provisions a machine is not, so the concepts are taught here and the YAML belongs next door.

    What you will be able to do

    Read a Pending Pod and say exactly which constraint blocked it. Set requests and limits deliberately rather than by copying a template. Predict what an HPA will do before it does it, and know why it sometimes does nothing. Choose between CPU, request rate and queue depth as a scaling signal. Explain why a node disappeared at 2am, and what a PodDisruptionBudget would have changed.

    Before you start

    No Kubernetes experience is assumed. Some of the reasoning is sharper if you have done the Observability course — lessons 3, 5 and 6 here all depend on choosing and measuring a metric, and they link back to it rather than repeating it.

    Every quoted claim is sourced on the references page. Progress is stored only in your browser. No tracking, no dependencies.
