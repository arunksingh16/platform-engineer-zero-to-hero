Lesson 2

Metrics: counters, gauges, histograms

Almost every metric you will ever meet is one of three shapes. Learn these and dashboards stop being magic.

By the end you can…

Pick the right metric type for a measurement, and build a histogram's bucket counts from raw numbers by hand.

1. Counter — only goes up

A counter counts events: requests served, errors, bytes sent. It starts at 0 and only increases (it resets to 0 only when the process restarts). Its raw value is rarely interesting; the rate of change is. Name them with _total, e.g. http_requests_total.

2. Gauge — goes up and down

A gauge is a current level: active connections, queue length, memory in use, temperature. Its raw value is the interesting part.

3. Histogram — how are values distributed?

Averages hide pain: ten requests at 10 ms and one at 10 seconds average ~920 ms, which describes nobody. A histogram counts how many observations fell into pre-chosen buckets. In Prometheus-style histograms buckets are cumulative: bucket le="0.5" ("less than or equal to 0.5 s") counts everything ≤ 0.5, including the faster ones. A histogram also stores _sum (total of all values) and _count (number of observations).

Rule of thumb

Counting events → counter. A level right now → gauge. Latency or size where you care about percentiles → histogram.
