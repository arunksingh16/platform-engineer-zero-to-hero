Lesson 9

SLIs, SLOs & burn-rate alerting

Telemetry is only useful if it answers "is the user happy?" and "should I wake someone up?". This lesson turns everything so far into those decisions.

By the end you can…

Define an SLI and SLO, compute an error budget, calculate burn rate, and explain why alerting on burn rate beats alerting on raw errors.

Three terms

  • SLI (Service Level Indicator): a measurement of user-visible quality, as a ratio of good events to valid events. Example: good = requests that returned non-5xx in < 500 ms, SLI = good ÷ total.
  • SLO (Service Level Objective): the target for the SLI over a window. Example: "99.9% of requests are good over 30 days".
  • SLA (Service Level Agreement): a contract with consequences (refunds). Looser than your SLO.

Error budget

If the SLO is 99.9%, you are allowed to fail 0.1% of the time. That allowance is the error budget. It is a feature: while you have budget, ship features and take risks; when it's gone, slow down and fix reliability.

✍️ Do it by hand — budget

  1. How many minutes are in 30 days?
  2. SLO 99.9%: how many minutes of full outage does the budget allow?
  3. SLO 99.99%?
  4. A service handles 10,000,000 requests in 30 days at SLO 99.9%. How many failed requests are allowed?
Show answer

1) 30 × 24 × 60 = 43,200 min. 2) 43,200 × 0.001 = 43.2 min. 3) 43,200 × 0.0001 = 4.32 min. 4) 10,000,000 × 0.001 = 10,000 requests.

Burn rate

Burn rate = how fast you're using budget compared with the "exactly on target" speed.

burn rate = current error rate ÷ (1 − SLO)

Burn rate 1 means you'd use exactly 100% of the budget by the end of the window. Burn rate 10 means you'd run out in a tenth of the window.

Burn-rate calculator

✍️ Do it by hand — burn

SLO 99.9%. Right now 1.44% of requests fail.

  1. What is the burn rate?
  2. How long until the 30-day budget is gone (30 days = 720 hours)?
  3. What % of the whole budget does one hour at this rate consume?
Show answer

1) 1.44% ÷ 0.1% = 14.4. 2) 720 ÷ 14.4 = 50 hours. 3) 14.4 ÷ 720 = 0.02 → 2% of the budget per hour. That's exactly the figure the Google SRE Workbook uses for its fast-burn page: 14.4× burn over 1 hour (confirmed with a 5-minute window).

Alert on burn, not on blips

"Alert if error rate > 1%" fires on harmless 30-second spikes and misses slow leaks. Burn-rate alerts ask: at this pace, will we miss our promise? A common scheme uses two windows so alerts are fast but not flappy:

Both windows must exceed the threshold: the long window shows it's serious, the short window shows it's still happening (so it clears quickly once fixed). In PromQL this is built from rate() and sum from lesson 5 over your SLI counters.
