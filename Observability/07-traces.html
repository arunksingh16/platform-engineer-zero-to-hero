Lesson 7

Traces and spans

A trace is the story of one request as it travels through your system. Each chapter of that story is a span.

By the end you can…

Read a trace waterfall, explain parent/child relationships, and calculate a span's self time by hand to find the real bottleneck.

Vocabulary

  • Trace — everything that happened for one request, identified by a trace_id.
  • Span — one unit of work (an HTTP handler, a DB query, a call to another service). It has a name, a span_id, a parent_span_id, a start time, a duration, a status and attributes (key/value details like db.system="postgres").
  • The span with no parent is the root span. Parent-child relationships form a tree in this basic model; OpenTelemetry span links can also associate spans without making them parent and child.

Self time — where the time really went

A parent's duration includes its children. To find the actual bottleneck, subtract them:

self time = duration − sum of (non-overlapping) child durations

If children run in parallel, subtract the time covered by their union, not the sum.

✍️ Do it by hand

Using the table above:

  1. Self time of the root span GET /checkout?
  2. Self time of payment.charge?
  3. Which service is the biggest contributor to the 500 ms?
  4. The auth.verify and inventory.reserve spans were changed to run in parallel (starting together). New root duration, if everything else is unchanged?
Show answer

1) 500 − (50 + 100 + 300) = 50 ms. 2) 300 − 260 = 40 ms. 3) The external call stripe.api at 260 ms (52%) — larger than any of our own code. 4) Sequential they cost 50 + 100 = 150 ms of the root; in parallel they cost only max(50, 100) = 100 ms of wall-clock time, so the root drops by 50 ms → 450 ms.

What traces are good and bad at

Good: finding which hop is slow, spotting N+1 query patterns, seeing fan-out, understanding dependencies.
Costly: tracing every request at high traffic is expensive, so systems use sampling: keep 1% at random (head sampling), or decide after seeing the whole trace (tail sampling, e.g. keep all errors and slow ones). You'll meet that in the advanced topics.
