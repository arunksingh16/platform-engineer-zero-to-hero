Lesson 5

Querying: rate, sum and percentiles

Raw counters are useless on a graph. Three operations turn stored samples into answers: rate, sum by, and histogram_quantile. We do the maths by hand first.

By the end you can…

Compute a per-second rate from counter samples (including across a restart), aggregate series by label, and estimate p95 latency from histogram buckets.

1. rate() — from a total to a speed

A counter says "1,500 requests so far". What you want is "how many per second right now". Take the increase over a window and divide by the window length. In PromQL: rate(http_requests_total[1m]).

rate() calculator

✍️ Do it by hand — rate

Counter samples every 15 s: 100, 130, 190, 250, 280.

  1. What is the total increase over the 60 s window?
  2. What is the per-second rate?
  3. Now the samples are 500, 520, 5, 25, 45. The app restarted between samples 2 and 3. What is the increase and the rate?
Show answer

1) 280 − 100 = 180. 2) 180 ÷ 60 = 3 requests/s.
3) Naively 45 − 500 = −455 would be nonsense. PromQL notices the drop (a counter reset) and treats the new value as counting up from 0: increases are 20 (500→520), then 5 (after reset: 0→5), then 20, then 20 = 65. Rate = 65 ÷ 60 ≈ 1.08/s. (Real Prometheus also extrapolates to window edges, so exact numbers can differ slightly — the idea is the same.)

2. sum by — combine series

You have one series per label combination. Aggregation collapses the labels you don't care about.

seriesrate (req/s)
{method="GET", status="200"}8
{method="GET", status="500"}1
{method="POST", status="200"}3
{method="POST", status="500"}2
  • sum(rate(http_requests_total[1m])) → 14 (everything)
  • sum by (method) (rate(...)) → GET 9, POST 5
  • Error ratio: sum(rate(...{status="500"}[1m])) / sum(rate(...[1m])) → 3 / 14 ≈ 21.4%

✍️ Do it by hand — aggregation

What is the error ratio for POST only?

Show answer

POST errors 2 ÷ POST total (3+2=5) = 40%. Even though overall the error ratio looks like 21%, POST is much worse — that's why we aggregate by labels.

3. histogram_quantile() — percentiles from buckets

p95 means "95% of requests were faster than this". With cumulative buckets we find the bucket that contains the 95th-percentile request, then linearly interpolate inside it:

estimate = lower + (upper − lower) × (rank − count_below) / (count_in_bucket), where rank = q × total.

Quantile explorer

Buckets (cumulative): le 0.1 → 50, le 0.5 → 90, le 1 → 100 (total 100)

✍️ Do it by hand — percentile

Same buckets (50 / 90 / 100 cumulative). Compute p95.

Show answer

rank = 0.95 × 100 = 95. Cumulative counts: ≤0.1 → 50, ≤0.5 → 90, ≤1 → 100. 95 lies in (0.5, 1]; 90 are below it and 10 inside it. Estimate = 0.5 + (1 − 0.5) × (95 − 90) / 10 = 0.5 + 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.75 s.
This is an estimate: Prometheus can't know where inside the bucket the values really are, so choose buckets around your SLO threshold.
